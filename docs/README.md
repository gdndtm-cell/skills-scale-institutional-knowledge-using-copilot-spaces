# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This README is an index to OctoAcme's project management guidance. The documents describe a shared, iterative approach to delivering customer and business value, with clear ownership, transparent decisions, and continuous learning.

## Project Management Process Summary

OctoAcme manages work through initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective closeout. Initiation validates the business need, defines measurable success criteria, identifies stakeholders, and records an initial timeline, risks, and resource needs. Once the initiative is approved, planning turns it into a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria, estimates, dependencies, milestones, a release plan, and a documented Definition of Done.

During execution, teams deliver in small increments and track work on a project board, using pull requests, regular demos, and team syncs to surface progress, blockers, and decisions. Project Managers coordinate delivery plans, risks, and communications; Product Managers define outcomes, prioritize the backlog, and measure success; developers design, implement, test, and review the work; QA/testing validates acceptance and quality; and stakeholders provide input and approvals. Risks and dependencies are captured, assessed, assigned owners and mitigations, monitored regularly, and escalated from the team through the PM and Product Lead to the sponsor when needed.

Communication is supported by daily delivery standups, weekly delivery and PM/Product alignment, milestone demos, stakeholder updates, and a shared source of truth for status. Quality is built into delivery with unit and integration tests, end-to-end smoke tests for critical flows, CI tests and linting, security scanning, and manual QA where needed. Releases require acceptance criteria and checks to pass, release notes and rollback plans to be ready, staging and post-deployment verification, and stakeholder communication. After sprints, releases, incidents, or milestones, retrospectives capture learnings and assign a small number of owned, time-bound improvement actions for follow-up.
